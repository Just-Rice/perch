How it works

A small app on your Mac sends its screen and sound straight to this page in your browser, and your clicks and typing go straight back. The two connect directly to each other, encrypted, the same way video calls in a browser do.

1

Install Perch on your Mac

One line in Terminal. Perch lives in the menu bar and starts by itself when you log in.

2

Pair your devices once

Perch's Setup shows a code on the Mac. Scan it, approve with Face ID or Touch ID, and add Perch to your authenticator app.

3

Use your Mac from anywhere

Open this site, sign in with your passkey and a 6-digit code, and your Mac is right there. Touch, mouse, keyboard and sound all work.

Install on a Mac

Open Terminal (in Applications › Utilities), paste this line and press Return:

curl -fsSL https://just-rice.github.io/perch/install.sh | bash

It downloads the latest Perch from GitHub, checks the download against the checksum published with it, puts Perch in your Applications folder and opens Setup. Setup walks you through the rest: two macOS permissions, your authenticator app and pairing your first device.

Why a Terminal command and not a download button?

Perch is free and isn't registered with Apple's paid developer programme. A Mac blocks unregistered apps that were downloaded in a browser ("Perch can't be opened because Apple cannot check it"). Files fetched by Terminal aren't marked that way, so the app simply opens. You can read exactly what the command does: install.sh.

Rather download it yourself? Get Perch.zip from the latest release, unzip it, move Perch to Applications and open it. When macOS refuses, open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway next to the message about Perch. Or build it from the code.

What you need
  • The Mac: Apple silicon (M1 or later) with macOS 14 Sonoma or later. Intel Macs aren't supported yet.
  • The device you view from: a recent Safari, Chrome, Edge or Firefox, on a phone, tablet or computer.
  • An authenticator app for the 6-digit codes: Apple Passwords, Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, 1Password and others all work.
  • The Mac has to be on, awake and logged in. Perch can keep it from going to sleep.
Uninstall

Open Perch's Setup from the menu bar and choose Uninstall Perch, or run curl -fsSL https://just-rice.github.io/perch/uninstall.sh | bash. Either way Perch stops starting at login, its settings and Keychain entry are deleted and the app goes to the Trash.

Private by design

This website is only the keypad. The lock is on your Mac: it checks every sign-in itself.

Two steps, every time

A passkey (Face ID, Touch ID or your phone), then a code from your authenticator app. Nothing is shown or sent until both pass. Five wrong tries locks sign-in for a while.

No servers in between

Your screen goes straight from your Mac to your browser, encrypted. Free public message services only pass along scrambled "here's how to reach me" notes, and can't read them.

You always know

The menu bar shows when someone is connected and macOS shows its screen-recording light. Only one device at a time, and you can end it from the Mac.

No passkey on the device you're using? Use your phone's passkey from it (most browsers offer "use a phone or tablet"), or turn on the optional backup sign-in: your Mac's name, a password and the 6-digit code. More in the privacy page and the security notes.

Only pair your own devices. Perch gives full control of a Mac. Nobody genuine will ever ask you to install it or to send them a pairing code; anyone who does is trying to scam you.

Good to know

  • It's a beta, tested on one Mac so far. Please report problems.
  • Very strict networks (some schools, workplaces and mobile carriers) can block direct connections. A relay option may come later.
  • Perch shows one display at a time; with several, you can switch between them.
  • It works inside an about:blank tab too: use the button at the top, or a launcher on this site such as About:Blank Lab. With no address showing, you can't check you're on the real site, so prefer your passkey to the backup password there.
