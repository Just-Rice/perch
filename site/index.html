How it works

Three steps, then it's just there.

A small app on your PC sends its screen and sound straight to this page, and your taps, clicks and typing go straight back. The two connect directly to each other, encrypted, the way video calls in a browser do.

  1. Install Roost on your PC

    One line in PowerShell. Roost sits in the tray, next to the clock, and starts by itself when you sign in to Windows.

  2. Pair your phone once

    Roost's Control Center shows a code. Scan it, approve with Face ID, a fingerprint or your screen lock, and add Roost to your authenticator app.

  3. Your PC, from anywhere

    Open this site, sign in with your passkey and a 6-digit code, and your PC is right there: touch, mouse, keyboard and sound.

What you get

Made for the device in your hand.

Phones first

Tap to click, hold for a right click, two fingers to scroll, pinch to zoom, or turn your screen into a touchpad. The Key deck keeps Win, Alt+Tab and Task Manager a thumb away.

Controls that keep out of the way

A small Dock floats over your PC's screen. Drag it to any edge or corner, or shrink it to a glowing orb. Every action is one search away in the Command palette.

Smooth on ordinary Wi-Fi

The picture is sized to your screen and your connection, with a 60 frames a second mode for fast links.

Copy here, paste there

Text and pictures go both ways between your device and your PC's clipboard.

Talk through your PC

Turn on your microphone and your PC plays what you say, on its speakers or into a call.

One window at a time

Show a single window instead of the whole desktop, and reshape it to fit the screen you're holding.

Show, don't share

Make a link that lets someone watch your screen for a while, without being able to touch anything.

Works on strict Wi-Fi

An optional relay gets through school and office networks that block direct connections.

Install

One line in PowerShell.

Right-click the Start button and choose Terminal (or open Windows PowerShell), paste this line and press Enter. No administrator rights needed.

>_Windows PowerShell— ▢ ✕
PS>irm https://just-rice.github.io/roost/install.ps1 | iex

Installing Roost for x64...
Checking the download... it matches
Roost is installed. It's starting now.

  • Windows 10 (1903+) or 11
  • x64 or ARM64
  • Any recent browser to view from

The line downloads the latest Roost for your PC's processor from GitHub, checks it against the checksum published with it, installs it just for you (in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Roost), sets it to start when you sign in, and opens the Control Center, which walks you through the rest.

Why a command, and not a download button?

Roost is free and isn't signed with a paid code-signing certificate. Windows SmartScreen stops unsigned apps downloaded in a browser with a blue "Windows protected your PC" screen. Files fetched by PowerShell aren't marked as downloaded from the internet, so Roost simply starts. You can read exactly what the line runs: install.ps1.

Windows protected your PCMicrosoft Defender SmartScreen prevented an unrecognised app from starting.
More info › Run anyway

Rather download it yourself? Get the zip for your PC from the latest release, unzip it and open roost.exe. If SmartScreen appears, choose More info, then Run anyway.

What you need
  • The PC: Windows 10 version 1903 or later, or Windows 11, with an x64 (Intel or AMD) or ARM64 processor.
  • The device you view from: a recent Safari, Chrome, Edge or Firefox, on a phone, tablet or computer.
  • An authenticator app for the 6-digit codes: Microsoft Authenticator, Google Authenticator, Apple Passwords, 1Password and others all work.
  • The PC has to be on, awake and signed in. Roost can keep it from going to sleep.
Update or uninstall

The Control Center tells you when an update is out. To remove Roost, run this in PowerShell:

irm https://just-rice.github.io/roost/uninstall.ps1 | iex

It stops Roost, stops it starting with Windows and deletes the app, and asks before deleting your settings. Read it first: uninstall.ps1.

Private by design

This site is only the keypad. The lock is on your PC.

Your PC checks every sign-in itself. Nobody behind Roost can see your screen, and there's no account to leak.

Two steps, every time

A passkey (Face ID, Windows Hello or your phone), then a code from your authenticator app. Nothing is shown or sent until both pass.

No servers in between

Your screen goes straight from your PC to your browser, encrypted. Free public message services only pass along sealed "here's how to reach me" notes they can't read.

You always know

The tray icon shows when someone is connected. Only one device controls the PC at a time, and you can end it from the PC.

Only pair your own devices. Roost gives full control of a PC. Nobody genuine will ever ask you to install it or to send them a pairing code; anyone who does is trying to scam you.

More in the privacy page and the security notes.

Good to know

The small print, in plain words.

  • It's a beta. Please report problems.
  • To click on apps that run as administrator (Task Manager, installers) and to see Windows' own permission prompts, turn on Control admin apps in the Control Center. Windows asks you once.
  • Windows never lets another program press Ctrl+Alt+Del or Win+L. Open Task Manager (Ctrl+Shift+Esc) from the Key deck instead.
  • Very strict networks (some schools, workplaces and mobile carriers) can block direct connections. The Control Center has an optional relay for those, using a free Cloudflare account of your own.
  • It works inside an about:blank tab too. With no address showing you can't check you're on the real site, so prefer your passkey to the backup password there.
